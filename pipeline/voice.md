# Stylodex voice

How every page reads. Modelled closely on Apart Style's voice guide and its finished listicles
(Ash, 2026-10-29: "could be more like Apart Style"). The *rules and moves* are adopted. None of
its text is, ever: no sentence, intro or "Best for" line is copied or lightly reworded from an
Apart Style page. Owner corrections on top are marked (Ash).

## Who's talking
A well-informed friend who shops a lot and has opinions, not a catalogue or a research report.
- "We" for our picks and opinions ("we'd start with…", "our pick if…"). "You" for the reader,
  always. Never "Stylodex recommends", never third person about ourselves.
- **Opinionated, and every opinion has a fact behind it.** "That's the reason most people end up
  here" is fine when the next clause says why (the price, the size range, the shipping).
- **Explain why a fact matters.** Not "prices are in pounds" but "prices are in pounds, so add the
  conversion and the shipping before you compare". Not "sizes XS to XL" but "sizes stop at XL,
  so this one's narrower than its price suggests".
- **Teach the reader one useful thing per brand** where the facts allow: which line to start
  with, what a collection name tells you, which pieces carry the price.
- **Honest trade-offs about a specific attribute** ("the range is wide, and that's also the
  catch: quality varies line to line"), never a verdict on the brand's character.
- **Compare picks with each other, not only with the anchor**: "if you want heavier denim, go to
  Represent instead".
- Contractions every time: it's, you're, don't, there's, they'll, you'll.
- A brand is "it", never "they".
- Warm and a little playful. Fine to start a sentence with "But" or "So". A colloquialism now
  and then ("the short version", "let's be real"), never more than one a page.

## What we never write
- **Process narration (Ash)**: "we checked", "Stylodex found", "on the day we checked", "this
  month", dates, "its About page says", "Wikipedia describes", "our checks were blocked". The
  method box and the "Last checked" line cover all of that. A fact we couldn't confirm is left
  out, not explained.
- **Spreadsheet sentences (Ash)**: a count or a ranking as the point of a sentence ("its 86 men's
  cotton tees start lower than Affliction's", "97 styles"). A ranking is fine only when the same
  sentence says why it matters: "the cheapest bras here, and for something you own five of,
  that adds up".
- **Verdicts on brands**: "no saint", "not perfect", "to be fair", "has its issues", "far from
  innocent", "let's not pretend". Criticise a specific claim with the evidence, or leave it.
- **Invented experience**: "I wore", "we tried", "feels buttery", "runs small". We describe
  what a brand makes and sells, never how it feels on.
- Hype and corporate-speak: "game-changing", "elevate your wardrobe", "must-have", "iconic",
  "high quality" with nothing behind it.

## Rhythm
- Mix short sentences (5–8 words) with medium ones (15–20). Nothing over 30 words. Never three
  sentences of the same length in a row.
- Paragraphs of 1–4 sentences, and vary them: a one-line paragraph between two longer ones.
  Don't end every paragraph on a punchy line.
- Two items beat three in a list. Rule-of-three is an AI tell.
- No semicolons. No em dashes. No bold or italics (the template is plain text).

## Prices and products
- Name real products from the page's product photos (`pipeline/data/product-images.json`) or
  the brand's own store, with the price straight after in parentheses: "the Loopy Eyelet Super
  Baggy Jeans (£80)". One or two per brand, more only if they each add something.
- Ranges read as words: "tees from $24.99 to $48.95", "most of it under $100".
- A store charging in another currency: its own price first, then "(about $93)".

## Page shape
- **Intro (50–80 words, Ash)**: open on a specific moment the reader recognises, not the brand
  name. ("You've found the perfect pair of Jaded jeans, then seen the shipping to the US.") Then
  why they're looking, then what the list gives them, sorted how. No process, no dates.
- **Each pick (110–170 words)**, in two or three short paragraphs:
  1. Answer first, self-contained: the brand's name, what it's best at for this reader, and a
     named product with its price. ("Represent is the closest thing to Jaded London's baggy
     denim made in…")
  2. The useful detail: which line or piece to start with, and what's alike or different from
     the anchor (fit, price, sizes, where it's made, shipping, returns) and why that matters.
  3. Optional, one or two sentences: the honest catch or who should skip it, pointing to
     another pick when one fits better.
- **Best for** (separate field, 5–12 words, lowercase start, no full stop): a use case, not a
  category label. "buying three pairs of baggy jeans for the price of one", "a 44 waist without
  leaving the look behind".
- **How to choose (80–130 words)**: a short decision guide across the picks by budget, fit or
  style, naming the picks. Concrete, different on every page, no generic CTA.
- **FAQ**: answers 15–50 words, answer first, no attributions or caveats.
